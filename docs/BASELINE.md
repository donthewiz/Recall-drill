`MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK`** change (see the dedicated section below) — the harness
itself changed here, not just the app: every config now runs **50 seeded
trials** and is reported as **mean ± sample SD** instead of one unseeded

## Notes

- `shortDeck`'s backs are all ≤3 words by design, so every card lands on the
  `full` stage (no chunking at all) — that's why its stage breakdown never
  shows `chunks`/`combine`/`remediate`, and why its numbers are identical to
  every prior phase's (nothing about B1/C1's chunking/ladder changes touches
  a deck that never chunks). This isolates the encode-vs-cycle cost
  independent of the chunking ladder.
- Phase 0's original baseline used the pre-C1 exhaustive ladder and had
  `proseDeck`/perfect at 240 trials; it's now 180 (see the Phase 3 section
  below for the full before/after).
- Phase 1 (B1/B2/B3/B5 bug fixes) and Phase 2 (C2 lenient grading) were both
  confirmed to leave the deterministic (`perfect`) learner's numbers
  unchanged, because `simulate.ts`'s learner always types either the exact
  target or an empty string and never produces a partial-miss or revealed
  answer — those fixes aren't exercised by this simulation's fidelity. Same
  is true for B1's fix specifically: the LCS-based culprit attribution only
  differs from the old positional one when a subset of words match, which
  `typed = ''` can't produce either.
- `realistic`/`struggling` rows are stochastic (`Math.random()`-driven);
  re-running `npm run simulate` reproduces `perfect` exactly but gives
  similarly-scaled, not identical, numbers for the other two.

## Phase 3 (C1) — forward-chaining ladder

`ladderMode: 'cumulative'` replaces the exhaustive `n(n-1)/2`-window ladder
with `n-1` growing prefixes (`{1,2}, {1,3}, ..., {1,n}`), and intermediate
windows only need 1 blind success instead of the full `encodeReps` (see
`requiredRepsForWindow`) — only the final (whole-answer) window needs the
full streak. `exhaustive` is kept available specifically so this comparison
can be measured, per the doc.

**Doc's acceptance target**: "Trial-count simulation shows ≥40% reduction on
the fixture deck for a perfect learner." Measured on `proseDeck`, perfect
learner, at several chunk difficulties:

| chunkDifficulty | Avg chunks/card | Exhaustive trials | Cumulative trials | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35% (SetupView's default) | 3.00 | 240 | 180 | 25.0% |
| 25% | 4.00 | 384 | 228 | 40.6% |
| 20% | 5.42 | 654 | 296 | 54.7% |
| 15% | 6.17 | 822 | 332 | 59.6% |

**Finding**: the ≥40% target is met once cards average ≥4 chunks — at
exactly 4 chunks/card the measured reduction is 40.6%, matching the doc's own
worked example (4-chunk answer, encodeReps=3: 12+18+2=32 → 12+5+2=19, a
40.6% reduction) to the trial, and at 6 chunks/card (59.6% here vs. the doc's
own 58.5% for a single 6-chunk card, both ≥55%) it's in the same range too.
`src/test/c1.spec.ts` verifies the exact 19-trial count end-to-end against
`applyAnswer` for a single 4-chunk card, not just the aggregate formula.

`proseDeck`'s fixture cards (17-22 words) only chunk into 3 pieces at the
app's actual default chunk difficulty (35%) — `chunkText`'s bucketing caps
around 3 chunks for that word-count range at that percentage (confirmed by
direct measurement of every card, not just the formula). That's why the
default-difficulty aggregate reduction (25%) reads lower than the doc's 40%+
examples: it isn't a shortfall in C1's implementation (verified exact by
`c1.spec.ts`, and the table above matches the doc's own per-card arithmetic
precisely once card chunk-count matches the doc's assumption) — it's that
35% difficulty produces smaller ladders to begin with, so there's
proportionally less exhaustive-ladder waste for forward chaining to cut.
This is a property of the fixture deck's word count interacting with the
default difficulty setting, not a defect in this phase; noted here rather
than adjusted, per the doc's own "measure, don't trust" framing — the
fixture wasn't hand-tuned to hit a target number.

## Phase 4 (C5) — cue fading replaces copy-typing

Attempt 0 of every stage-unit used to show the full target text in both
`subText` and `placeholder` (copy-typing). `selectTrial` now returns a
`Cue` (`{ kind: 'firstLetter', pattern }` on attempt 0, `{ kind: 'none' }` on
attempt 1+, unchanged) instead of deriving `isBlind` from the streak, and a
wrong verdict in the encode phase now returns `advance: 'manual'` instead of
auto-retrying on a dwell timer, so the diff is actually read and C2's
"Count as correct" override stays reachable indefinitely. Neither change
touches grading, streak thresholds, or `applyAnswer`'s branching — only what
gets shown and when the shell is allowed to move on.

**Doc's acceptance targets**, both structural rather than numeric:

- "`renderFirstLetterCue` preserves word count and punctuation position" —
  verified directly in `src/test/c5.spec.ts`.
- "a full-session simulation contains zero trials where the complete target
  text is visible before the first attempt" — true by construction now:
  `selectTrial` never returns `{ kind: 'full' }` (that variant only exists
  for SessionView's local reveal state), so every trial's cue is either
  `firstLetter` or `none`. `c5.spec.ts` walks a full chunks→combine→cycle
  session asserting exactly that.
- "a revealed-then-correct trial leaves the streak at 0" — already covered by
  Phase 1's B2 fix; unaffected by C5, re-exercised from the cue's perspective
  in `c5.spec.ts`.

**Trial-count effect (measure, don't assume — this table moves the opposite
way from C1/C2, and that's expected):**

| Deck | Learner | Total trials before C5 | Total trials after C5 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shortDeck | perfect | 60 | 60 | 0% (deterministic — see below) |
| shortDeck | realistic | 93 | 92 | ~flat (stochastic noise) |
| shortDeck | struggling | 148 | 139 | ~flat (stochastic noise) |
| proseDeck | perfect | 180 | 180 | 0% (deterministic — see below) |
| proseDeck | realistic | 247 | 384 | +55% |
| proseDeck | struggling | 510 | 732 | +44% |

**Why `perfect` is bit-for-bit unchanged:** `simulate.ts`'s learner always
types either the exact target or an empty string. Copy-typing an
always-visible target and typing the same target while seeing only a
first-letter cue cost the same keystrokes and both always succeed under
`perfectLearner`, so neither trial count nor keystroke count moves at all —
confirmed by the identical 60/385 and 180/12644 figures above. This is the
same "deterministic learner is blind to this fix" pattern noted for Phase
1/2's `perfect` rows.

**Why `realistic`/`struggling` go up, and why that's the intended tradeoff,
not a regression:** attempt 0 used to be free (copy-typing, modeled as
always correct). It no longer is — `realisticLearner`/`strugglingLearner`
now score a firstLetter-cue attempt at 0.75/0.5 base probability
respectively (still easier than a fully blind attempt, but not free — see
`test/simulate.ts`'s tunable comment). A miss on attempt 0 no longer costs
nothing: it resets the stage-unit's streak to 0, same as any other miss,
which is genuine extra practice, not wasted motion — this is exactly what
the doc's C5 rationale asks for ("[copy-typing] is transcription, not
retrieval, and generates near-zero learning"). The cost of removing a
learning-free step is that a non-perfect learner sometimes fails it, and
that shows up here as more trials, concentrated in `proseDeck`'s
`chunks`/`remediate` stages (the ones with the most attempt-0 opportunities)
— `remediate` goes from a minor contributor (121/510 struggling) to the
single largest stage (308/732), consistent with more chunk-level misses
now cascading into remediation.
- The Part 4 target table's "Total trials ≤ 45% of baseline" /
  "Keystrokes ≤ 50% of baseline" targets are cumulative across every phase's
  numbers relative to the *original* pre-Phase-1 baseline, not a per-phase
  target — see the table's own header in `docs/V2-HANDOFF.md`. C1/C2 moved
  the realistic/struggling numbers down from that original baseline; C5
  trades some of that back for zero-copy-typing, which the doc treats as the
  more important property ("roughly a third of all trials in the encode
  phase" were previously pure transcription). This isn't a "phase didn't
  move its metric in the expected direction, stop" situation — C5's own
  acceptance criteria are the structural ones above, not a trial-count
  reduction; the doc's cost table is scoped to C1/C2/C3 (the phases titled
  "the trial-count reductions" in Part 3), and C5 is listed separately.
- `realistic`/`struggling` rows are stochastic; re-running `npm run simulate`
  reproduces `perfect` exactly but gives similarly-scaled, not identical,
  numbers for the other two (same caveat as every prior phase in this file).

## Phase 5 (C3) — batch the encode phase

Encoding no longer drills one card to completion before touching the next.
`selectNextEncodeItem` round-robins over the current batch's not-yet-`ready`
items, rotating after every completed stage-unit (one chunk learned, one
combine window learned, one remediation span cleared) — the same event that
already triggered `advanceEncodeState` pre-C3, just now picking the next
*batch* item instead of always the first `new`/`encoding` item in deck
order. Once a batch's items are all `ready`, its cycle phase runs scoped to
just that batch; once all of a batch's items are `mastered`, either the next
batch's interstitial appears (`phase: 'batch-done'`, with "Next batch" and
"Save and stop") or, on the last batch, the session ends exactly as a
non-batched session always did. `batchSize` is optional on `SessionConfig` —
unset (or `<= 0`, or `>= deck size`) collapses to a single "whole deck"
batch, which is what every pre-C3 call site (this file's own scoreboard
config, `c1`/`c2`/`c5.spec.ts`'s single-item decks, the characterization
suite) still gets, unchanged, without being touched.

**Doc's acceptance criteria**, all structural rather than numeric —
verified in `src/test/c3.spec.ts`:

- "a 13-card deck at `batchSize: 5` produces batches of 5/5/3" —
  `partitionIntoBatches` test.
- "encode trial order within a batch shows at least one item switch before
  any item reaches `ready`" — walks a 5-card chunked deck and confirms more
  than one distinct item is visited before the first `ready`.
- "resume after 'Save and stop' restores the same batch and the same
  per-item stage state" — round-trips a `batch-done` state through a
  `SavedSessionState`-shaped JSON round trip (the way localStorage actually
  stores it) and confirms the reconstructed state matches exactly.

**A real bug found and fixed during manual verification, not caught by the
engine-level tests above:** resuming straight into a `'batch-done'` save
sent the learner directly to `DoneView`, skipping the interstitial
entirely. Cause: `SessionView`'s initial `useState` always seeds
`currentId: SESSION_COMPLETE_ID` as a placeholder for "pick a real trial on
mount," and every other phase's `initSession` branch (encode, cycle)
immediately overwrites it via `advanceEncodeState`/`advanceCycleState` --
but `initSession`'s `'batch-done'` branch deliberately returns the state
unchanged (there's no trial to select), so that placeholder survived
untouched. The session-complete `useEffect` only checked
`currentId === SESSION_COMPLETE_ID`, which the untouched placeholder still
matched, indistinguishable from "the whole session is over." Fixed by also
excluding `phase === 'batch-done'` from that check (`SessionView.tsx`).
This is exactly the kind of gap `c3.spec.ts` couldn't have caught -- those
tests drive `drillEngine.ts` directly and never touch `SessionView`'s React
state, so the fix and its regression risk live entirely in the UI layer.

**Trial-count effect, with `batchSize` left unset (whole deck) so this is
the closest apples-to-apples comparison to Phase 4's numbers:**

| Deck | Learner | Total trials before C3 | Total trials after C3 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shortDeck | perfect | 60 | 60 | 0% (deterministic — order-invariant) |
| shortDeck | realistic | 92 | 90 | ~flat (stochastic noise) |
| shortDeck | struggling | 139 | 146 | ~flat (stochastic noise) |
| proseDeck | perfect | 180 | 180 | 0% (deterministic — order-invariant) |
| proseDeck | realistic | 384 | 328 | ~flat, stochastic (down this run) |
| proseDeck | struggling | 732 | 935 | ~flat, stochastic (up this run) |

**Why `perfect` is bit-for-bit unchanged:** rotation only changes *which*
item's turn it is, never how many trials any individual item needs to reach
`ready` or `mastered` — those thresholds (`encodeReps`, combine window
counts, remediation) are untouched by C3. Summed across every item in the
deck, the total is order-invariant, so `totalTrials`/`keystrokes`/
`trialsByStage` all land exactly where Phase 4 left them (confirmed by the
identical 60/385 and 180/12644 figures above) — the same "a deterministic
learner is blind to this fix" pattern as every prior phase's `perfect` row.

**Why `realistic`/`struggling` move, even with `batchSize` unset:** unset
`batchSize` means "whole deck as one batch" (no interstitial checkpoint) --
it does **not** mean "go back to pre-C3 one-item-at-a-time presentation."
Rotation (round-robining over not-yet-`ready` items) applies regardless of
batch size; only the checkpoint is gated by `batchSize`. So even this
baseline's config now interleaves every card in the deck at stage-unit
granularity, which changes the *order* `Math.random()` is consulted in for
a stochastic learner -- same probability model, different draw sequence,
different outcome. This is the same category of change as Phase 4's
`realistic`/`struggling` shift (a structural change altering the random
walk, not the underlying per-attempt probabilities), and the doc's own
"measure, don't trust" framing is why this section reports the actual
numbers rather than assuming rotation is numerically neutral for anything
but the deterministic learner.
- `src/test/c3.spec.ts` covers the actual acceptance criteria (batch sizing,
  interleaving, resume fidelity) directly against the engine, where they're
  deterministic and don't depend on a learner model at all -- this
  simulation section exists only to keep the running trial-count scoreboard
  honest, per Part 4, not to re-verify C3's own behavior.

## Phase 8 (C8a) — chunk-stage criterion, part 1

The chunks stage's advance criterion changed from "chunkStreak reaches
encodeReps" to "one correct answer at cue level `'none'`", independent of
`encodeReps` -- every chunk now needs exactly one cued (`'firstLetter'`)
correct answer plus one blind (`'none'`) correct answer, never fewer, never
more, and never `encodeReps`-many. `encodeReps` still governs combine's
final window and the `full` stage exactly as before.

**Trial-count effect (config unchanged: `encodeReps: 3`, `batchSize` unset):**

| Deck | Learner | Total trials before C8a | Total trials after C8a | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shortDeck | perfect | 60 | 60 | 0% (no chunked cards at all) |
| shortDeck | realistic | 90 | 89 | ~flat (stochastic noise) |
| shortDeck | struggling | 146 | 161 | ~flat, stochastic (up this run) |
| proseDeck | perfect | 180 | 144 | 80% (chunks: 108 -> 72) |
| proseDeck | realistic | 328 | 305 | 93% |
| proseDeck | struggling | 935 | 796 | 85% |

**Why `shortDeck` doesn't move at all:** every `shortDeck` card is a short
(≤3 word) `full`-stage card with no chunking (see the Notes section above)
-- C8a only touches the chunks stage, so a deck with zero chunked cards is
untouched by construction, not by coincidence. This is the cleanest possible
confirmation that the change is scoped exactly where it claims to be.

**Why `proseDeck`'s `perfect` row shows the clearest signal:** `chunks:108`
-> `chunks:72` is an exact -33% on the chunks stage specifically, with
`combine`/`cycle` completely unchanged (48 and 24 both times) -- proseDeck's
12 cards average 3 chunks each at the default 35% difficulty (see the Phase
3 section above), so encodeReps=3's old "3 reps per chunk" cost (3 chunks x
3 reps x 12 cards = 108) drops to C8a's fixed "2 reps per chunk" cost (3 x 2
x 12 = 72) -- the arithmetic matches exactly, not approximately, which is
what you'd expect from a change that doesn't touch grading or the ladder
shape at all, only the chunk-advance threshold.

**`realistic`/`struggling` move less dramatically than `perfect`, and
`struggling` even moved the "wrong" direction on `shortDeck` this run:**
both are stochastic and unaffected by chunk-stage changes on `shortDeck`
specifically (no chunks there), so that row's shift run-to-run is pure RNG
noise, consistent with every prior phase's caveat here. On `proseDeck`,
`remediate`'s share of trials grew (`chunks:274/remediate:480` before ->
`chunks:157/remediate:469` after, for `struggling`) -- with fewer chances to
succeed at the chunk level before moving to combine, a struggling learner
reaches combine (and its miss-triggered remediation) sooner, which is
exactly the "error-driven allocation... is what the remediation system is
for" framing C8's own rationale describes, not a regression.
- `src/test/c8a.spec.ts` covers C8a's actual acceptance criteria (exactly 1
  cued + 1 blind per chunk regardless of encodeReps; combine's final window
  and the full stage unaffected; the `chunkStreak` migration clamp)
  directly against the engine -- this section exists only to keep the
  trial-count scoreboard honest, per Part 4.

## Phase 9 (C8b) — presentation trial replaces the cued chunk attempt

The chunks stage's attempt 0 (`chunkStreak === 0`) is no longer a graded
`'firstLetter'` typing attempt -- it's an ungraded presentation
(`cue: { kind: 'present' }`): the chunk's full text is shown, nothing is
typed, and acknowledging it (`verdict: 'presented'`) advances straight to
the first real, blind, graded attempt. It never counts toward
`stats.attempts` or `stats.misses`. This doesn't change how many chunk
*trials* there are (still 1 presentation + 1 blind per chunk, per C8a) --
only whether the first one costs any keystrokes.

**Trial-count effect (config unchanged: `encodeReps: 3`, `batchSize` unset):**

| Deck | Learner | Total trials before → after | Keystrokes before → after | Change (keystrokes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shortDeck | perfect | 60 → 60 | 385 → 385 | 0% (no chunked cards at all) |
| shortDeck | realistic | 89 → 79 | 562 → 515 | ~flat/stochastic (no chunks to affect) |
| shortDeck | struggling | 161 → 146 | 1030 → 923 | ~flat/stochastic (no chunks to affect) |
| proseDeck | perfect | 144 → 144 | 11204 → 9764 | 87% |
| proseDeck | realistic | 305 → 229 | 21116 → 15093 | 71% |
| proseDeck | struggling | 796 → 691 | 40572 → 34970 | 86% |

**Why `proseDeck`'s `perfect` row shows total trials completely unchanged
(144 → 144) while keystrokes drop:** this is exactly the acceptance
criterion in miniature -- C8b removes typing from a trial that was never
graded, not a trial from the sequence. `chunks:72` before and after,
identical; only what's typed for half of those 72 trials (the presentation
half) changed from "the chunk's full text" to nothing. The 1440-keystroke
drop (11204 → 9764 lines up closely, modulo the 2-chunk-vs-3-chunk mix
across proseDeck's 12 cards) with proseDeck's chunked cards' total
presentation-half character count -- confirmable exactly per-card via
`src/test/c8b.spec.ts`'s hand-walked comparison against `simulate()`'s own
output for a single two-chunk item.

**Why `realistic`/`struggling` show real trial-count reductions too, not
just keystrokes:** removing typing from the presentation half doesn't
directly reduce misses (a presentation could never be missed either way),
but it does mean `priorExposures` for a chunk's blind attempt now
accumulates purely from genuine blind-attempt history rather than being
diluted by a graded cued attempt in between -- `realisticLearner`/
`strugglingLearner`'s `pCorrect` still branches on `cue.kind === 'firstLetter'`
for other stages, but chunks no longer produce that cue at all, so their
per-chunk trial count reflects only the (harder, lower-`pCorrect`) blind
attempt's own retry statistics, not an inflated mix of two different cue
difficulties. `shortDeck` is unaffected either way -- no chunked cards.
- `src/test/c8b.spec.ts` covers C8b's actual acceptance criteria (presentation
  cue and verdict shape, zero attempts/misses/diff for a presentation,
  simulate.ts's zero-keystroke accounting verified against a hand-walked
  session) directly against the engine -- this section exists only to keep
  the trial-count scoreboard honest, per Part 4.

## Harness rework: seeded N=50 runs + mediumDeck fixture

Two fixes to the harness itself, prompted by two problems discovered while
using it across Phases 0-9:

1. **Seeding.** Every prior phase's table was a single unseeded
   `Math.random()`-driven sample. Re-running `npm run simulate` with no code
   changes at all could shift `realistic`/`struggling` numbers by a
   noticeable margin -- several "this phase made things worse" observations
   in earlier phase sections turned out, once measured properly (see below),
   to be within that noise, not a real effect. `test/prng.ts` (a small
   `mulberry32` PRNG + a string-hash seed derived from
   `"<engine>:<deck>:<learner>"`) and `test/simulate.stats.ts`
   (`runSeeded`/`meanSd`/`withinOneSd`) now run each config 50 times under
   deterministic, reproducible seeds and report mean ± sample SD.
2. **`mediumDeck` fixture** (`test/fixtures/mediumDeck.ts`, 12 cards, backs
   4-8 words -- the shape of a real terminology definition). Neither
   `shortDeck` (backs ≤2 words) nor `proseDeck` (backs 17-22 words) has a
   card anywhere near the 4-8 word range `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK`-style changes
   actually affect, which is exactly why that change was reported
   untestable in this file's previous entry.

Also added `test/simulateLegacy.ts`: drives
`src/test/reference/legacyEngine.ts` (the frozen pre-Phase-1 snapshot)
through the same seeded/N=50 machinery, using the exact original
(isBlind-based) learner models from `git show ccc4514:test/simulate.ts`, so
"Phase 0 vs final" is now two live statistical samples compared against each
other, not a live sample against a single old number frozen in this file's
git history. **Important caveat found while building it, documented in the
file itself:** `LegacyEngine` imports `buildItems`/`chunkText` and a few
other low-level helpers *live* from the current `drillEngine.ts` (deliberate,
so B1's fix is picked up automatically) -- so a "Phase 0" run is only valid
if taken *before* changing one of those shared helpers, not after. The
comparison below was taken before the `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` change; the
re-test after it (further down) deliberately does not use `simulateLegacy`
at all, for exactly this reason.

**Phase 0 baseline vs final, 50 seeded runs each, `shortDeck`/`proseDeck`
only:**

| Deck | Learner | Trials: Phase 0 | Trials: Final | Trials | Keystrokes: Phase 0 | Keystrokes: Final | Keystrokes |

instant* -- but it is not valid *now*, and it is not reproducible by
running the command this section tells you to run: `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` is
8 in the committed code, `simulateLegacy` reads `chunkText` live (see the
caveat above), and `mediumDeck`'s 5-7 word backs are exactly the range that
constant gates. Run `npm run simulate` today and the "Phase 0" column for
`mediumDeck` reads 60.0 ± 0.0 -- identical to Final, "NOT DISTINGUISHABLE"
-- because `LegacyEngine` picked up the new threshold too. Neither the old
231 nor the current 60 says anything about a Phase-0-vs-C1–C8 comparison for
`mediumDeck`; both are artifacts of whatever `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` happens to
be at measurement time, which has nothing to do with C1-C8's ladder or
grading changes. The section below (`MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` = 8) has the
comparison that's actually valid for `mediumDeck` -- current engine against
itself, before and after the constant changed, both sides measured with

**The `shortDeck` finding is the important correction here.** Every prior
phase section in this file (Phase 3, 4, 5, 8, 9) already labeled `shortDeck`'s
`realistic`/`struggling` single-sample deltas "stochastic noise" in their
own tables, but the prose in those sections still implied a live mechanism
was moving the number, phase over phase. Measured properly, **none of those
`shortDeck` rows are statistically distinguishable from Phase 0 at all, for
any learner.** Precisely which phases could even mechanistically reach
`shortDeck` matters here, and the claim needs to be exact: C1 (combine
ladder) and C8a/C8b (chunk-stage criterion, presentation trial) are
chunk/combine-specific and genuinely cannot touch a deck with zero chunked
cards -- that part was always right. But **C2 (lenient grading) and C5 (cue
fading) are not stage-specific** -- `grade()`'s near-miss tier and
`selectTrial`'s cue-derivation both run for the `full` stage exactly as they
do for chunks, so both mechanisms are genuinely live on `shortDeck` too, in
principle. The finding is not that C2/C5 structurally can't reach
`shortDeck` (an earlier draft of this section overstated that) -- it's that
their effect on this specific fixture (1-2 word answers, essentially no
stopwords to drop and little room for a first-letter cue to matter) is too
small to clear the noise floor at N=50. `proseDeck`, by contrast, is
unambiguously, robustly distinguishable in the expected (reduced) direction
on both metrics, for every learner -- that's the real, load-bearing effect
of C1/C2/C5/C8's ladder and grading changes, on a deck where those
mechanisms have enough surface area (chunked, multi-word answers) to move
the number past sampling noise.

## `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` = 8 (kept)

Previously reported as untestable: neither `shortDeck` nor `proseDeck` has a
card in the 4-8 word range this threshold change (chunkText's guard, was 3)
actually affects. Re-tested against `mediumDeck` (backs 4-8 words, added
above) specifically, per the decision rule: keep if `mediumDeck` improves by
more than one SD, revert otherwise. This is a **current-engine-vs-itself**
comparison (`MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK: 3` vs `8`, both post-C8), not a Phase 0
comparison -- see the caveat above about why `simulateLegacy` can't be used
to evaluate a change to a helper it shares live with the current engine.

| Learner | Trials before (=3) | Trials after (=8) | Diff vs. max SD | Keystrokes before | Keystrokes after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| perfect | 141.0 ± 0.0 | 60.0 ± 0.0 | 81.0 vs 0.0 -- improves | 3211.0 ± 0.0 | 2455.0 ± 0.0 |
| realistic | 259.9 ± 28.7 | 92.0 ± 6.2 | 167.9 vs 28.7 -- improves | 5513.0 ± 528.4 | 3763.5 ± 249.5 |
| struggling | 682.1 ± 73.4 | 149.4 ± 12.6 | 532.7 vs 73.4 -- improves | 11846.5 ± 1185.8 | 6107.0 ± 530.6 |

Every learner improves by roughly 6-19x the relevant SD -- nowhere close to
the 1-SD line. **Decision: keep.** `mediumDeck`'s 12 backs (5-7 words) all
chunked before this change and all land on the `full` stage after it --
2-3 fewer stage-units per card (no chunk trials, no combine window) is a
real, structural reduction, not noise.

**Attribution, stated explicitly since it's easy to conflate with the rest
of this file:** this win belongs entirely to the `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK`
constant, not to C1-C8. It's a fixture-shape effect -- a back either clears
a fixed word-count bar and skips the ladder, or it doesn't -- with no
dependency on the forward-chaining ladder, lenient grading, the chunk-stage
criterion, or anything else built in Phases 1-9. Nothing here changes the
per-chunk or per-window cost for any answer that *does* still chunk (a
`proseDeck` card, for instance, is exactly as expensive per chunk as it was
before this constant existed). Treat `MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` as an independent,
separately-tunable knob, not evidence for or against any of the C1-C8 work.

`shortDeck`/`proseDeck` are
unaffected by construction either way (confirmed by their `perfect` rows
being bit-for-bit identical before and after in the main table above).
`src/test/chunkText.spec.ts` covers the exact boundary
(`MIN_WORDS_TO_CHUNK` itself, and that a back at or under it never chunks
regardless of `chunkDifficulty`) directly, and `src/test/fixtures/deck.ts`'s
`TWO_CHUNK_BACK`/`TWO_CHUNK_DIFFICULTY` were updated (9 words at 50%, still
exactly 2 chunks) since the old 5-word version fell under the new
threshold and stopped chunking at all.
